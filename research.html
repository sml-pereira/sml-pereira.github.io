Research

My research develops the Fourier Qualia Space (FQS), a framework that uses Fourier transforms to follow how harmonic qualities such as chromaticity, octatonicity, diatonicity, and whole-tone quality unfold over time and build hierarchical and syntactic structure. At its heart is qualia ambiguity: rather than dismissing it as analytical noise, the FQS treats it as a structural resource that composers use to shape form and tension.

The repertoire spans four centuries of Western art music, with a soft spot for the post-tonal works of Schoenberg, Debussy, Webern, and their neighbours, explored through both large-scale corpus analysis and close reading. Ambiguity, it turns out, is far easier to enjoy once you can measure it.

Articles

Statistically Modelling Qualia Syntax Through Fourier Space: A Three-role Probabilistic Framework Where Ambiguity Becomes Structure

Pereira, S., Bernardes, G., & Oliveira Martins, J. Musicae Scientiae.

A statistical framework for characterising how harmonic qualities play statistically-endorsed syntactical roles in Debussy's music using Zipf's law and transitional matrices within Fourier space.

Qualia Motion in Fourier Space: Formalizing Linear, Nondirected and Contrapuntal Ambiguity in Schoenberg’s Op. 19, No. 1

Pereira, S., Bernardes, G., & Oliveira Martins, J. Music Theory Spectrum 48(1).

Introduces a formal typology of harmonic-quality ambiguity in Schoenberg’s Op. 19, No. 1 through the definiton of the Fourier Qualia Space: linear, nondirected, and contrapuntal.

FluidHarmony: Defining an Equal-Tempered and Hierarchical Harmonic Lexicon in the Fourier Space

Bernardes, G., Carvalho, N., & Pereira, S. Journal of New Music Research 51(2–3): 142–61.

Proposes an algorithmic method for defining a hierarchical harmonic lexicon in any equal-tempered tuning system, tested on five centuries of Western music.
